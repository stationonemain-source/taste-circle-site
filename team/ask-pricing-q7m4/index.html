Ask Taste Circle: a real test, and what to charge

We ran it on Jaime’s real account with Claude Haiku. Here is how fast it was, how often it was right, what it costs us, how we could price it, and what else the same photo notes can do.

For Circle, Nate and Jaime. Nothing is built in the app yet.

WHAT ASK IS

Describe a dish. Ask finds it in your own posts.

1

You describe it

In your own words, like you’d tell a friend. No dates or names needed.

2

It looks only at your plates

Never anyone else’s. It reads the photos too, not just the captions.

3

It shows you the plates

The best match first, then the related ones, each with its date and spot.

HOW IT WORKS

Two steps, both on Claude Haiku

ONCE PER PHOTO, IN THE BACKGROUND

Describe the photo

When a plate is posted, Haiku writes a short note: the dish, what’s in it, homemade or restaurant, and where it was eaten. Nobody waits for this.

EACH TIME SOMEONE ASKS

Narrow, then pick

A plain word search cuts your plates to the likely ones, for example only sandwiches. Haiku then reads those notes and picks the matches.

AFTER THE FIXES

No mistakes in 3 runs

Every day, count and spot checked by hand. 2 to 4.5 seconds and $0.0003 to $0.0005 a recap. About $3 a month at 1,000 users.

The rule we learned: anything with a number or a date in it comes from the code, and Haiku only does the writing.

BEFORE IT GOES LIVE

Six guardrails

Opt-in first

Apple requires asking before photos go to an AI company, naming it (Anthropic).

Claude only

Google’s terms bar its AI models in apps likely used by under-18s. We’re rated 13+.

Your plates only

The server takes who’s asking from their login, so nobody can search someone else.

A spending limit

A monthly cap per user and on the account, with a Discord alert.

The “students never pay” rule

Taste Circle+ breaks it. The team has to decide to change it before we sell anything.

No body talk, no hidden ads

No calories, weight or dieting, since users can be 13. Every paid pick says Sponsored.
